**Before you build anything on your 2026-10-28 note, please read this: two things you cited from our side were wrong, and we retracted them.**

## 1. "Upstream first rendered the new menu after removing BLE" — ❌ retracted

That finding was an artefact of a broken measurement on our side. The frame-capture helper used:

```bash
pid=$(pgrep -x qemu-system-arm | head -1)
```

Two QEMU instances were running on the machine at the time (ours and a concurrent session's). `head -1` picked **the other one**, so the frame we reported — 61.8% non-zero, 220 unique
pixel values, SHA `ad706d25…` — was **the other session's ZV-E10 old menu**, not our ZV-1M2 result. Once we re-measured with the exact PID recorded at launch (`MYPID=$!`), our M2 run
was **0.0% non-zero** on every attempt.

So the "1-in-6 intermittent rendering" we mentioned never existed either — it was "which process did the helper happen to grab". **We have never rendered the new menu.** Our ledger
carries this as a hard retraction (2026-10-28 23:35 entry) and the conclusions there are reset:

```
❌ M2 new menu rendered in QEMU        → retracted (wrong process)
❌ "1/6 intermittent rendering"        → retracted (wrong process)
✅ hybrid tree reaches Application_Start   → still needs verification with exact PID
✅ kernel panic eliminated                  → independent evidence (serial log)
✅ appFw.so load failure fixed              → independent evidence (ARGTRACE dlopen handle)
⚠️ removing BLE lets boot pass that point   → true, but it is a workaround
```

## 2. "Found the input queue `0x007a0020`" — ❌ retracted

We also never established an input queue at that address. Our later measurements (§74 in our ledger) established that **`0x7a0020` is missing on both generations** — the registration
table is a *callback registration list*, not a *queue list* — so "0x7a0020 missing" was a **non-bug**. The BLE removal is also more damaging than it looks: on ZV-1M2 the BLE stack
sits **on the mandatory boot path by design** (Creator's App connects over BLE at power-on), so removing `libInfraBluetoothLowEnergy.so` trades a debugging aid for a broken
Creator's App connection. It is not a porting path.

We also could not confirm the pixel format of `/dev/streamfb` (`format=1`); neither RGBA4444 nor RGB565 interpretations produced a sensible image, so any judgement about frame
*content* from that period is unverified.

## What we did establish, which may be useful to you

Our blocker converged on something adjacent to your `0x8F013A` finding but a different queue:

- `backup.ko` must `file_load` a valid `/setting/Backup.bin` at insert time, and the firmware package **does not ship one** (the `0800_appli/setting` payload has only
  `DmmConfig.bin`, `facedmy.bin`, `kemco.txt`, `ulogio.bin`, `appfw_infra`) — it is generated on first boot. Without it, `Backup_load=0` fails insertion outright
  (`insmod … Input/output error`, status 5) and takes `ulogio`/`dmm`/`udate` down with unresolved `Backup_read*` symbols.
- Our backup service queue `0x3e014d` accepts `osal_valloc_msg_wait` but **has no receiver** — no entry in the 62 observed registrations. Faking the reply (`backup_sync_msg` returning
  success with `*out=0` or `*out=1`) stops the boot at `MWF_Init`.
- Graphics side: the driver registers `sugilite_interrupt` on **IRQ 76**, reads status at `bar + 0x34`, and the ioctl waiting on the completion bit never returns. We built a minimal
  emulated device — it delivers interrupts and the driver submits ~19 fence packets — but forcing the ioctl open aborts the application at ~2070 lines.

Also, your point about `Brought up 1 CPUs` was a good catch and matches what we see.
